SABLE has to answer one question: which allowed week should each exam get? It must spread exams evenly, stop two exams in the same class from ever landing in the same week, and keep every exam clear of holidays and deadline periods.

The worked example below uses fixed weekdays — Math ×4 on Wednesday and English ×3 on Thursday in class 5M — so you can trace every number by hand. Since the update, each row can also use ✨ Auto (any Mon–Fri, most even spread) or split days per exam (e.g. 1st = Monday, 2nd = Wednesday). The core rule never changes: every single exam gets its own calm-date list, a target position, and then claims its nearest free week.

Three day modes · one engine

Case A — Fixed: one weekday per row (e.g. always Wednesday). Case B — Auto: any Mon–Fri, about 5× more dates, therefore the most even spread. Case C — Per exam: exam 1 = Monday, exam 2 = Wednesday, exam 3 = Auto … each exam searches only its own weekday list.

1

Find the “calm” dates

The first job is to throw away every week an exam is not allowed to use. Which weekday counts depends on the day mode — the function candidatesFor(wd, …) builds one list per exam, and wantDay(row, i) tells it which weekday exam i wants (0 = ✨ Auto, 1–5 = Mon–Fri).

How the three cases build candidates

Case A — Fixed (wd = 3): take every Monday of the year, add wd − 1 days to reach Wednesday, keep the date only if it is inside the school year, past the teaching buffer, and its week is not blocked. Result: one date per week, e.g. 26 Wednesdays.

Case B — Auto (wd = 0): same filters, but emit all five weekdays per Monday. Result: about five dates per week (e.g. ~130 calm days). More choice means the greedy step lands closer to its ideal position — that is why Auto spreads most evenly.

Case C — Per exam (row.wds = [1, 3, 0]): exam 1 builds a Monday-only list, exam 2 a Wednesday-only list, exam 3 an Auto list. The engine stores candsByExam[row][examIndex] — a separate list per exam, not one list per row.

Why this matters

The two lists line up almost index-for-index — position 2 is Wed 12 Nov for Math and Thu 13 Nov for English, the same week. That means a date's index (its position in the list) is a good stand-in for “how far through the year” it is.

2

Give every exam a target position

Each exam gets a target fraction between 0 (start of year) and 1 (end of year). With n exams the fractions are simply 0, 1/(n−1), 2/(n−1) … 1. That fraction is then converted into an index into that exam's own list: idealIdx = fraction × (list.length − 1). A fixed-Wednesday exam indexes into 26 dates, an Auto exam into ~130, a per-exam Monday into ~26 — the formula is identical, only the list differs.

3

Merge and sort into one queue

All seven exams go into a single shared list, ordered by target fraction. If two exams tie on fraction, the sort breaks the tie by of — the number of exams that subject has — so the subject with fewer exams goes first.

The tie-break in action

English 1/3 comes before Math 1/4 even though both sit at fraction 0.000. The second sort key, of, sends the subject with fewer exams first. Without it, Math would always grab the very first week.

4

Place each exam on its nearest free week

Walk the queue top to bottom. For each exam, scan its own still-free dates, measure the distance |index − target|, and claim the smallest. A claimed week (keyed by its Monday) is removed from the pool for good — which is exactly what enforces “one exam per class per week”, no matter which weekdays the exams wanted. An Auto exam can dodge into Tuesday to avoid a clash; a fixed Wednesday exam can only slide to another Wednesday.

The two subjects alternate instead of Math finishing in October and English starting in June. No two exams ever share a week, because that week leaves the pool the moment it is claimed.

The one imperfection

Math 1/4 was pushed a full week late (index 1 instead of 0) because English took the first week on the of tie-break. Total error across all seven exams is only 1.67 index steps out of 51 — so the schedule is already very close to ideal.

How to explain this in one breath

Each exam is assigned a target position between 0 and 1 across the school year, which is then converted into an index into that exam's own list of allowed dates — one weekday list for fixed mode, all weekdays for Auto, or a different weekday per exam for split mode. All exams from all subjects in a class are sorted by that target position, so they get placed in chronological order and different subjects naturally interleave. Each one then claims the nearest free week from its own list, and since claimed weeks are removed from the pool, the one-exam-per-class-per-week rule is satisfied automatically.

Numbering rule

Single-day rows (A + B) are renumbered chronologically after placement, so exam 1..n always follows date order — a collision that pushes an exam a week late simply swaps its number. Per-exam rows (C) keep their authored numbers, because “Exam 1 = Monday, Exam 2 = Wednesday” must stay tied to the right date even if placement order shifts. The app shows the wish next to each result as a gold chip (✨ Auto or Mon…Fri).

Why Auto wins

Error is measured as |chosen index − ideal index|. With 5× more candidates, the nearest free date is on average 5× closer to the ideal. Fixed exams can only slide whole weeks; Auto exams can also slide within the week (Tue instead of Wed) to dodge a blocked week without losing evenness.

Example to try in the app

Set Math to ✨ Auto ×4 and English to split ×3 → Mon / Thu / Auto, then press Generate. Math will land within a day or two of its ideal positions, English 1 stays Monday, English 2 stays Thursday, English 3 (Auto) fills whichever weekday keeps the class clash-free.
